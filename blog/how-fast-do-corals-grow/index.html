How fast do corals grow?

By Sander Bos · 20 November 2026

The honest answer: it depends, and the numbers people quote disagree by a factor of three or more.

I went looking for solid growth rates and found mostly shop guides and forum threads. There is very little measured data for home aquariums, so treat every number below as a rough guide. What I can add is what growth looks like in photos over time, from my own tank.

SPS corals (Acropora, Montipora)

Retail guides give conservative figures. One puts staghorn Acropora at about 4 to 8 inches (10 to 20 cm) a year in good conditions, and branching Montipora at 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) a year. Hobbyists on forums often report faster growth, around an inch (2.5 cm) a month for healthy SPS, and some quote up to 2 inches a month for the fastest ones. A figure often repeated from Borneman's Aquarium Corals is a fastest recorded Acropora rate of about 18.5 cm a year, or 1.5 cm a month.

Put together, a healthy, fast SPS colony might add anywhere from about 10 cm to about 30 cm a year. Where yours lands depends on the species, the light, the flow and the water. Two things come up again and again:

  • Frags start slowly. A new frag spends its first weeks recovering and encrusting onto its plug. Bigger colonies grow faster because they have more tissue making skeleton.
  • Flow changes the shape. In strong flow a colony tends to grow more compact instead of taller, so "growth" is not only length.

LPS corals (hammers, torches, Goniopora)

I could not find reliable numbers for these, and I would not trust anyone who gives you one. What the hobbyist reports have in common is the pattern. Growth comes in bursts. One reefer described a hammer with a few heads that looked unchanged for four to six months and then divided and nearly doubled in two months. Another pointed out that LPS do not necessarily grow slower than SPS; the change just becomes visible once there are more heads dividing. Occasional feeding is often said to help, and shops mostly recommend medium light and moderate flow.

Softies and zoanthids

I have no sourced figure here at all. Zoanthids and other soft corals spread by adding polyps, so "how fast" is better counted in polyps per month than in centimetres, and it varies a lot between colonies.

What growth looks like in real photos

Numbers on a page are hard to feel. These are three corals from my own tank, photographed over time and kept in Polyp.

Looking at these, a pattern shows that is easy to miss in daily life: you cannot see growth from one day to the next. You see it from one month to the next, or one season to the next. Most of the change happened in the gaps between photos, when it looked like nothing was happening.

How to measure your own

  1. Take a photo when you place the frag. This is day 1.
  2. Photograph it from the same spot and angle every two to four weeks.
  3. Put something of known size in the first photo, such as the plug or the disc, so you can compare later.
  4. Write down changes to the tank (new light, new feeding, a move) so you can tie a burst of growth to a cause.
